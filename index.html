BEFORE THE CHECKPOINT

Close the slides

You have just read through Part 1 and seen the loop drawn out. Before you answer anything, map it onto an example the slides never walked through. It takes about five minutes and it is the habit this module is built around.

THE STRATEGY

Retrieval practice

Pull the information out of memory before you look at it again. Rereading tells you whether something seems familiar. Retrieving tells you whether you can produce it, which is what an exam and a patient both require.

This is the one strategy that feels worse and works better. Expect it to be uncomfortable. That discomfort is the work.

Map it onto sweating

The situation. A healthy adult is working outdoors on a hot afternoon. Within minutes they are sweating heavily and their skin is flushed.

Fill in each stage of the loop for this client, from memory, without scrolling back. Short answers are fine. The gaps are the point.

1Set pointNormal desired condition
HOW IT MAPSCore body temperature, held near 98.6 degrees Fahrenheit, or 37 degrees Celsius. The body defends that range continuously whether the person notices or not.
2DeviationA change occurs away from the set point
HOW IT MAPSCore temperature rising above the set point. Heat from the environment and heat generated by the work are both adding to the load. The direction of the deviation is upward, and that decides everything that follows.
3SensorDetects the deviation
HOW IT MAPSThermoreceptors, in the skin and centrally in the hypothalamus. They register that temperature has risen above the defended range.
4Control centerCompares input to set point and determines the response
HOW IT MAPSThe hypothalamus. It compares the temperature being reported against the set point, registers that the value is above the range, and calls for heat loss rather than heat generation.
5EffectorCarries out the instruction
HOW IT MAPSSweat glands and the blood vessels in the skin. The glands secrete fluid onto the surface. The vessels dilate, which is why the skin is flushed.
6Response opposes changeThe response counteracts the original deviation
HOW IT MAPSRemoving heat rather than adding it. Sweat evaporates off the skin and carries heat with it, and dilated surface vessels radiate heat outward. The deviation was a rise, so the response produces a fall. That opposition is what makes the loop negative feedback.
7Return toward set pointConditions return toward the normal range
HOW IT MAPSCore temperature falls back into the normal range, and the sweating and flushing ease off because the sensors are no longer reporting a deviation. The loop does not switch off. It goes quiet and stays ready to run again.

Fill in all seven first, even roughly.

WHAT THAT TOLD YOU

These stages did not come back

    These stages only, not the whole loop. Nothing is saved, so write them down somewhere you will see them again.

    Why this works

    The retrieving itself builds the memory. Every time you successfully pull an answer out of your head, you make it easier to pull out the next time. Rereading does not do this. It strengthens how familiar the page looks, not how available the knowledge is, which is why material can feel clear while you are reading it and vanish the moment something asks you to produce it.

    Struggling to recall is not a sign this is going badly. It is the thing working. A stage you had to dig for will hold better afterward than one that arrived instantly, because the digging is what strengthens the path back to it.

    A stage that would not come at all is information. It told you what you do not have yet, which rereading would have hidden behind a feeling of familiarity.

    Carry it forward

    The version you use in your own course is simple. Whatever you just read, close the book and write down what you can recall. Whatever will not come is what you study next, before you read the entire block again or read something new. Then close the book and try again.

    Now go back to the slides and complete the checkpoint. You are about to meet this loop running in the opposite direction. Commit to an answer before you look at the feedback, the same way you committed to these.
